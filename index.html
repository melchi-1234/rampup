What a day looks like

A plan built from what you actually got wrong

Four rules decide your day, in this order. You can see why each block is there.

1

Clear what is due for review

Questions you missed come back on the day you are most likely to be forgetting them. Postponing review is the most expensive habit in spaced repetition, so it goes first.

2

Work on a pattern RampUp has spotted

Not a vague nudge — a measured one. "You've missed acid–base 4 of the 9 times it came up this week," or "you're at 78% on standalone Physics questions and 41% once there's a passage."

3

Learn something new to you

For a Preview subject, the concept lesson comes before the first question — you are never tested on material RampUp knows you have not been taught.

4

Sit a timed exam when it is time

Seven of them: two full-length 230-question simulations across four timed sections, two half-lengths, and three short timed sets — all run in an interface styled after the real testing software: highlighting, strikethrough, flag-for-review, section review screens, and the periodic table, the way you'll actually meet them on test day. Every exam also exports as a printable PDF.

The proof it is working

Rampy explains the question you just missed

Rampy is a coach that sits inside the question, not a chatbot bolted on the side. When you get something wrong it already has the question, the answer you chose, the correct answer, and RampUp's own written explanation — so you never have to re-type or re-explain anything.

Why was mine wrong?

It answers about the specific choice you picked and the misconception it points to, not the question in general.

Content, or reasoning?

It can tell you whether you missed the concept or misread the passage — different problems with different fixes.

Another on the same idea

It finds a real, verified question from the bank on the same concept rather than inventing one.

Rampy answers from RampUp's own explanations rather than improvising. That constraint is deliberate — it is why its answers agree with the material you are being graded against.

Questions

What exactly is the 511+ Guarantee?

Score a 511 or above, or your money back. Answer at least 1,000 questions in RampUp, complete both full-length practice exams, then take the real MCAT. If your official score comes in below 511, email us your AAMC score report within 60 days and we refund 100% of the subscription fees you paid — a score below 511 is the only score that qualifies. Each claim is reviewed individually against those conditions. The full terms are in the Terms — five short paragraphs.

Can I see my starting levels before paying?

Yes. The onboarding is free and needs no account and no card. You answer a few questions about your coursework and see a real level for every subject plus your first day's plan. The subscription is what unlocks the practice itself.

Is this a replacement for AAMC official material?

No, and you should sit the official practice too. RampUp is original material written to match MCAT structure and level, aimed at the stretch before and between official full-lengths. We are not affiliated with the AAMC.

Will RampUp predict my score?

Only from a timed full-length or half-length sitting, and it is clearly labelled as an unofficial estimate. We deliberately do not project a score from everyday practice: the difficulty adapts to you, so that number would mostly measure the adapting, not your ability.

What happens to my progress if I cancel?

Your progress is stored on your own device, so it stays there. Access to the questions, exams and Rampy ends when the subscription does.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up and no login. Your progress lives in your browser or on your phone, which is also why it does not follow you between devices automatically.

I bought exams from RampUp before. What happens to them?

You keep them, permanently. Anything bought under the old one-time pricing stays unlocked and is not affected by the subscription.
